## Proposal (one intervention)

- **Claim:** `H-013`.
- **Factor:** `G` (verification and governance).
- **Change:** `M-019` — run the existing payload probe as a gate at the start of `scripts/weekly.sh`.
- **Design:** before/after, labeled quasi-experimental. The gate must stay on, so it is not toggled between phases; the limitation is reported rather than laundered.
- **Metric:** `undetected_delivery_lapses` — weekly runs that compute metrics while at least one mapped rule is missing from the payload.
- **Baseline:** 1 lapse (this run).
- **Sample:** 8 weekly cycles.
- **Falsifier:** a lapse reaches the metrics step without the gate failing, or the gate fails while every assertion is true.
- **Stop rule:** 8 cycles or 2 false alarms.
- **Cost:** one small provider request per week, about 10 seconds; no new dependency, no new detection logic.
- **Rollback:** delete the gate step from `scripts/weekly.sh`. The probe stays for manual use.

Observed behavior: the gate exits non-zero on the current payload (5 false assertions, 6 load errors) and the weekly job aborts before the reviewer. The assertion branch was exercised against a fixture probe; the load-error branch fired independently of the probe.

Tradeoff, stated plainly: while delivery is broken the scheduled job now refuses to measure, so the lapse must be recorded by hand. That is the intended refusal — a weekly report computed against an undelivered harness is worse than no report — but it moves incident capture from the loop to the operator.

## Limits

- 15 sessions, one user, one week; every rate here is coarse.
- Trace-level labels are missing, not zero. The metrics table reports absence rather than guessing.
- The gate covers only what the probe asserts. An extension can fail while no assertion covers it.
- The reviewer is still uncalibrated (`EXP-0012`); its labels are drafts until checked against human labels.
